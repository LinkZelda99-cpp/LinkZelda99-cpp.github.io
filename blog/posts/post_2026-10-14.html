Building My Developer Portfolio From the Ground Up

Published October 14, 2026

Programming has always been a passion of mine. Over the years, I've worked on many projects that have helped me grow as a developer, and I eventually realized that I needed a way to showcase my work. That's when I discovered GitHub Pages and decided to create my own developer portfolio.

The Idea

The goal was to create a central place where I could display my projects and make them easy for others to find and appreciate. I wanted a website that would bring my work together in one place and present it in a way that reflected me as a developer.

Designing the Website

One of the biggest inspirations for my website's design was a photograph my dad took back in 2022. It has dark colors and a moody atmosphere, and I wanted to bring that same feeling into the website's overall design. That very photograph is now featured on the homepage.

I also wanted the website to be easy to navigate, so visitors could quickly find the information they were looking for. I went with a simple layout, clear headings, and distinct sections. I also included links to my social media profiles and contact information to make it easier for people to connect with me.

Building It

I started building the website in WebStorm, which is a great IDE for web development. My first file was index.html, and at the beginning, the website had no styling at all. It was just a tiny, plain page.

From there, I gradually built on what I had. I introduced CSS to style the page and began developing the website's visual identity. Working with just two files, index.html and style.css, taught me a lot about web development. I learned about Flexbox, cards, and how to bring different elements together to create a polished design.

I also learned about responsive design and the importance of making a website look good across different devices. As the site developed, I added images, including the mountain photograph on the homepage. I then built the header and footer that are now used throughout the website.

Eventually, I added a blog section, which is where you're reading this post! Along the way, I learned a lot about HTML and CSS, as well as the importance of testing, debugging, and refining my work.

Overall, building this website has been a great learning experience, and I'm proud of what I've accomplished so far.

What's Next?

I'm going to continue updating and improving my website as I learn more about web development. I'll also keep adding projects and blog posts as I work on them.

I have more features planned, including a contact form and more detailed project sections where visitors can explore my work. There's still plenty I'd like to do, and I'm excited to see how the website develops and how the experience helps me grow as a developer.
